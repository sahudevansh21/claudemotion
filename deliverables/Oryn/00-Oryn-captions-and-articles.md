# Price is not a verdict

A price feed answers one question: what is this token worth? Whether it is safe to act on is a different question, and a price alone can't answer it.

A tokenised stock can show a perfectly normal price while the exchange is closed, while a split has not been applied on one chain, or while a fund's redemption window is shut. To a price-only integration, every one of those looks fine.

Three examples from the Oryn board:

**xTSLA on Solana is Divergent.** Stock split 3:1 not applied on Solana. The token's onchain state no longer matches the issuer's.

**BUIDL on Ethereum and Arbitrum is Redemption closed.** The fund redemption window is shut; collateral cannot be redeemed at published NAV. The window opens in 6h.

**dMSFT on Arbitrum is Stale.** Arbitrum head is not advancing. This is not a market closure, so it should not be treated as a weekend freeze.

None of these is a pricing problem, and none of them shows up in a price. Each one is a question about whether it is safe to act right now.

Oryn collapses that question into one value per token, per chain, with the reason and the evidence beside it, so you can branch on a single value instead of a pile of fields. And when Oryn can't tell, the rule is simple: unknown means not actionable.

### A2 · Article (short) · How to read a frozen price
**Caption**
Flat price? Check why. Market closed and Stale look the same on a chart and mean opposite things.
#Oryn #RWA #OnchainRisk

**Article (full text)**

# How to read a frozen price

Two things look identical on a chart: a flat line because the market is closed, and a flat line because the chain stopped. They call for opposite responses.

**Market closed.** When the underlying exchange is closed, the oracle price is frozen at the last close, as expected. Nothing is wrong. oNVDA on Ethereum and Base and oQQQ on Ethereum show this today, with the market opening in 6h. dMSFT on Base is the same: Market closed.

**Stale.** When a chain's head stops advancing, the price stops moving for a very different reason. dMSFT on Arbitrum is Stale: Arbitrum head is not advancing. Not a market closure; do not treat it as a weekend freeze.

Same token, same flat line, two different verdicts. One is a normal pause that ends when the market opens. The other is a chain problem that you should not wait out on the assumption that it is a weekend.

A chart alone cannot separate them. Oryn does: one verdict per token, per chain, with the reason alongside, so a frozen price is never mistaken for a broken one, or the other way round.

Oryn answers the second. One verdict per token, per chain, with the reason beside it: Redemption closed for BUIDL, Open for USTB. You see the window, not just the number.

# A bond is not an asset

A bond proves skin in the game, not that an offchain asset exists.

Tokenised assets promise exposure to something offchain: a share, a fund unit, a treasury. An issuer can post collateral or a bond to show commitment, and that is useful. It is not proof that the underlying asset exists, or that what is onchain matches what the issuer holds.

Those are two separate questions, and only the second can be checked continuously: does the onchain state match the issuer's state, right now?

That is the question Oryn is built around. For each token on each chain it compares the two and returns a single verdict. **Open** means the onchain state matches the issuer: redemptions open, NAV fresh. **Divergent** means it doesn't, for example a 3:1 stock split that has not been applied on Solana. And where Oryn can't tell, it doesn't guess: unknown means not actionable.

A bond can show that an issuer has something to lose. A verdict shows whether it is safe to act on the token in front of you.
